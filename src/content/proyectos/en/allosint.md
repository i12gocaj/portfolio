I tried it on friends and family, and they were all surprised. Things came up that they didn't remember posting. We checked, and yes: they had posted them.

AllOsint is my master's thesis and one of the most ambitious and enjoyable projects I have built. I chose Eduardo Sánchez, who specialises in OSINT, as my supervisor, and the idea was his: a platform designed for police use that, starting from an email or a phone number, gathers what open sources know about a person.

And they know far more than you would think.

It starts with data breaches. If your email has appeared in one, the passwords you used in the past show up, and with them the awkward question of whether you are still reusing any of them today.

Then it goes through 54 services to see where you have an account. Sometimes it only confirms that you exist. Other times it returns your avatar, the date you signed up or details you would never think say anything about you: if you have an active streak on Duolingo, we already know you are learning a language.

If the email is a Gmail address, the reviews you have left come up. And a handful of reviews of bars, shops and gyms is enough to place where you live with great precision.

With the phone number it checks whether you have WhatsApp, Signal or Telegram. With the nicknames that appear in the breaches it searches hundreds more services. And every new email or phone number that turns up along the way goes back into the investigation, until it reaches other accounts of yours or people connected to you.

Everything runs on the investigator's own machine, and it doesn't sell certainties it doesn't have. A positive result is a signal, not proof of identity. When a source doesn't respond or is inconclusive it says so, instead of treating it as a negative. And at the end it lets you choose which results go into the PDF report.

None of this is magic or privileged access. It is information that is already out there, within reach of anyone who knows where to look, and AllOsint only brings it together. That is why it is designed for a police investigation and not for anyone's curiosity, and should only be used on your own data or with explicit authorisation.

It is an extremely powerful tool and I enjoyed building it enormously. It is worth taking a look.
