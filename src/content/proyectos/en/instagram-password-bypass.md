This story begins in a hospital in Romania.

During my Erasmus in Iași I was admitted with pharyngitis. That is a separate story, because there is plenty to tell there too, but you can get the idea: a public hospital, the infectious diseases ward and a room shared with eight other people. Great fun.

Anyway, I was bored. And when I'm bored I do odd things, like changing the passwords of every service I use. Instagram's turn came.

Changing a password properly has its little ritual. First they ask for the current one, then the new one twice, in case you mistype it. Asking for the current one seems redundant, since you're already logged in, but it makes complete sense: if someone picks up your unlocked phone, they shouldn't be able to lock you out of your account without knowing the password you had.

Well, I typed the current password wrong. And I knew it. I pressed next anyway.

Instagram accepted the change without any trouble.

I checked. I logged out, tried the new password and got in. I ran a thousand tests and they all ended in the same place: Instagram wasn't checking the current password. The flaw was so big that you could type a single "a", when no password can possibly be one character long.

A really silly bug, the kind that needs no tools or knowledge, just a mistake. And that is exactly why it was serious: anyone with your session open for a minute could take over your account.

---

I reported it to Meta and they rejected it. They told me they trusted their account recovery systems, and so on and so forth. I appealed and they rejected it again. I showed them with videos. Nothing.

Weeks later, the trick stopped working. They had fixed it without a word.

I suppose admitting I was right cost them more than fixing it quietly. Or that it was a little embarrassing to acknowledge something like that had slipped through. I don't know, because they never told me.

It's all documented in my repository, with the report, their replies and the proof video, so you can see this really happened even if it's hard to believe. I found it hard to believe too, and I had it right in front of me.

A month later, another bored afternoon led me to [Instagram Notes](/en/projects/instagram-notes/). But that's another story.
