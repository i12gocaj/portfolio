BrillanteSeguro is one of the projects I have been most excited about.

I live in an area where burglaries are very frequent, and they often break in while people are at home. Because of that, a WhatsApp community was created so we could warn each other if someone was in danger.

Over the months the community grew and channels appeared: one for second-hand buying and selling, another for street incidents so the administrator could pass them on to the city council, another to announce lost animals. And with the growth came the problems.

The important things got lost among thousands of messages. People talked so much during the day that, when you wanted to know whether something serious had happened, you weren't going to read all of it. For the administrator, handling all that at once was madness.

And something worse was happening: data was being lost. If I say today that I'm selling a sofa and a hundred messages from other people follow, whoever is looking for a sofa won't find it. If I say today that I've been burgled, a week later nobody remembers. Nobody connects one warning to another, and they may have been breaking in around the same area all week without us noticing, while the neighbours on those streets carry on calmly thinking it was a one-off.

So I wanted to build a website for the community.

It brought together in one place what already existed in the channels: burglaries, street incidents and pets. At the centre, a map to see at a glance what is happening in the neighbourhood. You click on a pin and see the details, with photos and a description. You filter by what interests you. You turn on the heatmap and see where reports cluster over the period you choose. And the administrator can manage it all quickly.

There was a statistics section designed to show the police what is happening, where and since when, so that something could be done or patrols arranged. Even to get ahead of it: if a week before a run of burglaries other kinds of incidents tend to appear in that same area, there is a pattern there that lets officers be warned about what is likely to happen.

And there was the neighbourhood side. A Wallapop-style marketplace, where what someone posted could be searched for later, and a services board to find a trusted plumber or gardener when needed.

---

This story ends a little sadly.

More than 400 neighbours signed up. They did it through an association membership form I built so nobody had to download or fill in anything by hand. You signed with AutoFirma or with a handwritten signature validated by a one-time code, the PDF filled itself in, the data was sorted into an Excel sheet and everything arrived in the community's inbox. The administrator had the members' register up to date without doing anything.

But hardly anyone ever used the platform for what it was designed for.

Why? Because of a flaw I didn't consider until I had finished building it. People use what they are used to, and that is WhatsApp: fast and convenient. Nobody wants to open a website, log in and fill in several fields to say there was a burglary on such-and-such street yesterday. Even less so when we are talking about older people, who aren't used to running their day-to-day through a new tool.

That was the key mistake. BrillanteSeguro died almost as soon as it was born.
